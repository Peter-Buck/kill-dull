FAQ

QUESTIONS. ANSWERED.

01

Why the name KILL DULL?

Because dull is expensive.

It makes companies easier to ignore, easier to replace and harder to care about.

And dull rarely comes from one obviously bad decision. It accumulates through reasonable choices, safe compromises and things that seemed harmless at the time.

We make those choices harder to make without noticing what they cost.

02

AAH. HMM. DULL. Isn’t that a bit basic?

Yes. Deliberately.

The work behind a judgment can be complicated. The judgment itself shouldn’t be.

AAH. There is something worth protecting or pursuing.
HMM. There is reason to hesitate.
DULL. Something important is being weakened or lost.

The reasoning can run deep. The answer still has to be understood.

03

What exactly is BENCH?

BENCH is independent scrutiny for consequential marketing decisions before you commit to them.

It interrogates the decision, the evidence behind it, the assumptions underneath it and what it could do to the company over time.

Then it makes a recommendation.

You make the decision.

04

What kinds of decisions belong on BENCH?

Decisions that can materially change what the company means, does or becomes.

Positioning. Brand architecture. Naming. Launches. Partnerships. Market entry. Identity-shaping customer experience decisions. Consequential technology choices that affect how the company is experienced.

A useful test:

Will this still matter after the meeting is over?

If yes, it may belong on BENCH.

05

What doesn’t belong on BENCH?

Things that are inexpensive to change and unlikely to alter the company.

A headline. A layout. A social post. A minor executional choice.

BENCH scrutinizes commitments, not assets.

Not every decision needs BENCH.

06

Isn’t this what a CMO is supposed to do?

Yes.

BENCH doesn’t replace the CMO’s judgment. It gives that judgment independent scrutiny before the organization commits.

Important decisions elsewhere in business are routinely subjected to independent challenge precisely because the people making them are capable.

Independence is not a substitute for leadership.

It is something leadership can use.

07

Who are you to judge?

We’re not asking you to hand us the decision.

We’re asking you to let us challenge it.

BENCH brings a defined process, independent scrutiny, evidence, experience and a recommendation.

The client keeps the final judgment—and the accountability that comes with it.

08

What makes the judgment independent?

We have nothing to sell after the answer.

We don’t execute the decision. We don’t produce the work. We don’t earn more because a recommendation gets approved.

A no is as successful for us as a yes.

Independence isn’t about being outside the company. It’s about being outside the outcome.

09

Isn’t this just another consultancy?

No.

Consultancies are generally paid to develop recommendations and often to help implement them.

Kill Dull stops before implementation.

We scrutinize the commitment. We make our recommendation. Then we leave the decision with the people responsible for making it.

10

What if BENCH is wrong?

It can be.

Judgment is not certainty.

BENCH exists to make the reasoning better: expose assumptions, examine evidence, identify consequences, surface disagreement and make a clear recommendation.

We would rather make uncertainty visible than disguise it as confidence.

11

Doesn’t this slow decisions down?

Sometimes.

The better question is whether the decision is worth slowing down.

A reversible decision made quickly is one thing. A commitment that changes what customers expect, what employees repeat or what future teams inherit is another.

The more expensive the undo, the more valuable the scrutiny beforehand.

12

Can a perfectly sensible decision still be wrong?

Yes. That is one of the problems we are built around.

A decision can improve efficiency, reduce cost, follow the data and make complete sense on its own—while making the company less distinctive overall.

The individual decision may be rational.

The accumulation may not be.

Good decisions can still add up wrong.

13

What do you mean by drift?

Drift is what happens when a sequence of reasonable decisions gradually makes a company less itself.

A small exception becomes precedent. Precedent becomes normal. Normal becomes the starting point for the next decision.

Nobody decided to change the company.

It changed anyway.

14

Isn’t consistency supposed to prevent drift?

Consistency can prevent change.

It can also preserve the wrong thing very efficiently.

We care more about coherence: whether different decisions, made at different times and in different forms, still add up to something recognizably yours.

15

What is a Dense Idea?

A Dense Idea keeps a company recognizably itself while everything around it changes.

Not a tagline. Not a campaign. Not a set of guidelines.

An idea dense enough to help you decide what belongs—and what doesn’t.

16

Isn’t a Dense Idea just a brand platform?

A brand platform usually describes the brand.

A Dense Idea has to help run it.

Put it in front of a difficult decision and it should make some choices feel more right, others more wrong, and a few impossible.

If an idea cannot help you choose, it isn’t doing enough work.

17

What does AI do?

AI assists throughout the process. It can investigate, compare, challenge assumptions, find contradictions, examine alternatives and connect evidence.

But it cannot become accountable for the consequence.

AI assists. Humans judge. The client decides.

18

Who makes the final decision?

You do.

BENCH makes a recommendation. The client retains the authority to accept it, reject it or do something else entirely.

The person or organization living with the consequence should retain the authority to decide.

Judgment stays human.

19

What’s the difference between BENCH and PITCH?

They intervene at different moments.

BENCH is for a consequential marketing decision before you commit. Should we change the positioning? Change the brand architecture? Enter this market? Launch this product? Make this partnership?

It asks: Should we do this?

PITCH is for making the work better. It takes you through four stages:

BRIEF — Interrogate the problem before you solve it.
PLAY — Open up possibilities before you narrow them down.
DESIGN — Make the strongest idea stronger.
LEARN — Turn what happened into an advantage next time.

BENCH scrutinizes the commitment. PITCH challenges the making.

20

What do I actually get from BENCH?

A clear recommendation and the reasoning required to understand it.

What the decision strengthens. What it weakens. What assumptions it depends on. What the evidence supports. Where uncertainty remains. What it could teach the organization to approve next.

Enough depth to deserve the recommendation.

Enough clarity to use it.

22

And what is that worth?

For BENCH, look at what is at stake.

A consequential marketing decision can shape years of investment, customer expectations and the decisions that follow it. The cost of getting it wrong rarely arrives as one invoice. It accumulates.

A $40,000 BENCH on a $20 million commitment is 0.2%.

The question isn’t what scrutiny costs. It’s how much you’re prepared to commit without it.

For PITCH, the economics are different.

A weak brief, an obvious first answer or an idea polished too early can cost far more than the tool used to challenge it.

At $59 a month for Pro, one better decision can pay for a lot of months.

The cheapest time to make the work better is while you can still change it.
